# D-0002: The inside corner is vanilla's climb, and the ceiling is the north wall tipped back

Rusty, the evening of 2026-09-23, with 0.1.0 on the box: "seems like wall redstone does
not corner", then "Just test em both" (an L on one wall and two walls meeting), then "Oh
it should also work on the underside of blocks, and corner upwards/downwards as well."

What was actually wrong. Every corner *worked*: the L on one wall and both two-wall
corners passed on a real server, and photographs of the L on all four walls were right.
What did not was the *drawing* of every inside corner, the turn from one plane onto the
plane at right angles to it: a floor run ending at the foot of a wall with the wall run
above it, or a wall run meeting the next wall. Power crossed, one point down as at every
wire-to-wire step, but the floor line stopped a block short of the wall and the wall line
started at the floor with nothing joining them, because the two wires are neighbours
through a face vanilla has no joint for (the block in front of the wire). A run that
turns a corner without drawing one looks broken, whatever the lamp says.

Accepted:

- **The inside corner is vanilla's climb.** Vanilla draws floor dust climbing the face of
  a block beside it when dust sits on top of that block: the `UP` joint, a band up the
  block's face to the cell edge. The inside corner is the same picture. A wire whose
  *front* cell (toward its up) holds a wire resting on the block beside it, toward planar
  side `p`, draws `p` as `UP` (`SIDE` when that face is not sturdy, as vanilla's climb
  does), and counts that wire as a neighbour for power. The band on the face meets the
  other wire's own line at the cell edge, so the run is continuous through the turn. This
  is `Wire.connectingSide` and `Wire.targetPower` over a `View` that now carries
  `frontRestsOn`, the planar side the wire in front rests toward. It covers every pairing
  of planes: floor to wall (both ways), wall to wall, wall to ceiling and ceiling to wall,
  in both orders of which cell is the corner cell.
- **A wire facing this one across a gap is not a neighbour.** 0.1.0 counted any wire in
  front for power (that is how a floor run ending in front of the lowest wall dust joined
  it). Two walls one block apart with dust on each, or a floor circuit under a ceiling
  circuit in a two-high room, would have cross-connected. Now only a wire in front that
  rests on one of this wire's side blocks counts; the front-and-facing case is out of
  reach, as it is between two floor circuits with air between.
- **Vanilla's floor dust joins in through two more injects, not a rewrite.** Floor dust at
  the foot of a wall is vanilla's block and knows nothing of the wall dust above it. An
  inject at the head of `getConnectingSide` returns `UP`/`SIDE` toward the block the wall
  dust above rests on, and an inject at the return of `calculateTargetStrength` takes the
  wall dust above into the max, one point down. Both fire only when the block above is
  wall dust resting toward a horizontal, so vanilla's own behaviour is untouched elsewhere.
  With the accessor of D-0001 that is four touches on vanilla, all in one mixin class.
- **The ceiling is the north wall tipped back over your head.** `Frame.CEILING` has up =
  world down, support = world up, TOP = north, BOTTOM = south, LEFT = east, RIGHT = west:
  lying on your back with your head to the north, north is beyond your head and east on
  your left, which is exactly the north wall (stood before, facing south, east on the
  left) rotated back until it lies over you. Chosen because that is the one x rotation
  (`x=90`) of the north-wall dust models, so the ceiling reuses every wall model with no
  new quads, and the diodes solve the same way. A ceiling is otherwise no different from
  a wall: the same blocks, `FACING`/`WALL` now admitting `down`, placed by the same items
  when the clicked face is a block's underside (the torch's rule again: the inward
  direction of the clicked face, `UP`, is tried first), rotated and mirrored by carrying
  each joint's world direction through the turn (a wall's LEFT/RIGHT swap under a mirror
  falls out of that; a ceiling's joints are absolute).
- **Reported through the booth, not the tests alone.** The first corner report was a
  drawing defect the gametests could not see: they check joints and power, and the joints
  *were* consistent with vanilla's rules as stood up. The photo booth now includes the
  stair (floor, wall, ceiling, round the roof's edge to a lamp) and the two-wall corner,
  photographed from where the seams show. A gametest per corner asserts the `UP` joint
  now, so the drawing rule cannot regress silently, but the photograph is the evidence.

Rejected: a new joint kind or property for the front (vanilla's `UP` is the same picture
and needs no new models); teaching vanilla's dust the corner through `canConnectRedstone`
(it is asked about sides, never about the block above); a mirror-image ceiling frame with
west on the left (needs mirrored models: a rotation cannot mirror); connecting a wire to
one facing it across a gap (surprising, and no floor analogue).
