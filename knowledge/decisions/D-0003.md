# D-0003: A wall diode also reads the block it hangs on

Rusty, with 0.2.0 on the box, standing in front of it: "The wall mounted redstone repeater
doesn't seem to be powered properly." Read off the server: a row of blocks with dust running
along its top, and a repeater hanging on the row's face just below the edge, its input the
empty cell above it and its output down into the ground; off. A second one the same, with a
torch standing on the row instead of dust.

Under D-0001 the block a wall diode hangs on was its floor, and a floor repeater standing on
a powered block ignores it, so the repeater was behaving exactly as on a floor. But a wall's
"behind" is ambiguous in a way a floor's is not: the builder sees the dust run to the edge
right above the repeater's back, the classic "dust into a repeater", and the block that
touches the repeater's back is precisely the block the dust powers. Told the two options
(keep the floor rule and use a floor repeater in front of the row; or let a wall diode read
the block it hangs on), Rusty: "Yes, 2. I thought that's how it would have been already."

Accepted:

- **The support block is a second input.** `WallDiodeBlock.getInputSignal` returns the
  stronger of the in-plane input (the block toward FACING, a wire's power counting as
  vanilla counts it) and `level.getSignal(support, towardSupport)`, the same call vanilla
  makes for any block behind a repeater. So the block a wall repeater or comparator hangs on
  feeds it when that block is strongly powered: dust on top of it, dust of either kind
  pointing into it, a torch under it, a repeater pointing into it. It is vanilla's strong-power
  semantics applied to the one block a wall diode touches that a floor diode does not.
- **What does not change.** A torch standing on the block does not feed a diode hanging on
  it, because a torch never powers the block it is attached to (Rusty's second repeater stays
  off, as it would on a floor). A comparator still reads containers only in the plane, behind
  it along the wall; the support block gives it redstone power, not an analog reading. The
  output still goes only toward FACING's opposite. A diode's output never lands on a support
  block (outputs lie in the cells in front of the wall, supports inside it), so no diode feeds
  itself through this rule.
- **Pinned by the builder's own geometry.** `SupportGameTests` builds Rusty's step: a floor
  run climbing onto a raised block, the repeater on that block's far face with its output
  down into the ground, a lamp beside the ground block. Dust on top: on, and off again when
  the source goes. Wall dust into the block from another face: on. A torch on the block: off.
  A comparator in the repeater's place: on. The booth photographs the step lit.

Rejected: keeping the floor rule and documenting the floor-repeater workaround (Rusty's
expectation is the builder's, and the geometry is the most natural way to hang a repeater
under a run); reading the support only when the in-plane input is empty (a max is simpler
and monotone, and no case distinguishes them); reading a container the comparator hangs on
(a floor comparator does not read the chest it stands on; not asked).
