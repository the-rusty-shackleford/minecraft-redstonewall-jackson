# D-0001: A wall circuit is the floor circuit turned, in new blocks placed by the vanilla items

Rusty, 2026-09-23: "make redstone dust, repeaters, etc applicable to walls. They should
present and work exactly as if they were on a floor, but its a wall." Then, on the
preview: "Can the mod not just make normal redstone stuff also apply to walls?" and, told
why vanilla's own blocks cannot be taught a wall, "alright proceed your way". The four
calls in the preview were taken as recommended.

Accepted:

- **The frame.** A floor circuit is a plane with an up (world up), four planar directions
  (the horizontals) and a support (down). A wall circuit is that plane stood up: up is the
  wall's outward normal, the planar directions are world up, world down and the two
  horizontals along the wall, support is into the wall. `domain.Frame` names them TOP,
  BOTTOM, LEFT and RIGHT as a person facing the plane sees them. Every rule is written once
  against a frame and reproduces vanilla on the floor frame.
- **Vanilla's wire rules, pure.** `domain.Wire` is `RedStoneWireBlock`'s joint, shape,
  power and signal logic re-expressed over a `View` (the neighbourhood already turned into
  the frame), so the "same as the floor" claim is a JUnit fact, not a hope. The adapters
  (`Views`, `WallRedstoneWireBlock`) read the level and apply the result; the diodes port
  `DiodeBlock`, `RepeaterBlock` and `ComparatorBlock` method for method with their
  directions taken from the frame (`domain.Diode` gives the sides and the placement facing).
- **New blocks, not vanilla's.** A block's state properties are fixed at registration and
  vanilla's wire and diodes have no face property; nothing can be added to a vanilla block,
  and rewriting their methods through mixins is the same work with worse odds. So three
  blocks: `wall_redstone_wire` (FACING is the wall's normal; TOP, BOTTOM, LEFT, RIGHT joints;
  POWER), `wall_repeater` and `wall_comparator` (WALL the normal, FACING the input direction
  in the plane). The comparator keeps its output in vanilla's own `ComparatorBlockEntity`,
  subclassed only to report this mod's own entity type (NeoForge's event for adding blocks
  to an existing entity type insists the block extend `ComparatorBlock`, which ours cannot).
- **The vanilla items place them, and they drop the vanilla items.** No new items, tab
  entries or recipes. A `UseItemOnBlockEvent` handler in the item-after-block phase follows
  the torch's rule: the directions the placer looks along, nearest first, with the clicked
  face's inward direction ahead of them; DOWN means vanilla's floor form (left to vanilla if
  it can stand there), a horizontal means the wall form on the block that way if it can hang
  there, UP is skipped. Clicking a wall face gives the wall form, clicking a floor the floor
  form. A repeater's output points where the placer looks, projected onto the wall.
- **The seam.** Where a wall run meets a floor run, power crosses like any wire-to-wire step,
  one point down. Three geometries: a floor run ending in front of the lowest wall dust (the
  wall dust counts the block in front of it as a wire neighbour; a floor wire never has
  one); a wall run above a floor wire directly below it (a planar neighbour, nothing
  special); a floor run on the wall's top edge (vanilla's own step-down rule, which reads
  wire power through a class check). That last one is a mixin: an inject at the head of
  `RedStoneWireBlock.getWireSignal` returning wall dust's power. A wire gives another wire
  nothing through a signal, since wires read each other's power directly; a wall wire
  answers a wire asker with zero. And vanilla silences every wire while any wire computes
  its power, through a private flag on its block, so no wire reads itself or another back
  through a strongly powered block; wall dust shares that silence in both directions through
  an accessor onto that flag, the mod's second and last touch on vanilla. Without it the wire
  above a comparator read its own power back through the wall and stuck at the higher value,
  and the wall-top seam leaked full strength both ways: the first gametest run showed both.
- **Look.** Vanilla's textures and colour. Wall dust's parts are flat quads generated for a
  north wall and turned to the others by a blockstate y rotation; the diodes reuse vanilla's
  model files, with a pre-turned copy for the facings a blockstate's x-then-y rotation
  cannot reach (`devtools/models/generate.py`, which also verified the game's rotation
  conventions against the ladder and the lever).

Rejected: mixins overwriting vanilla's blocks (see above); one wall block for every
vanilla block with a face property added (the state tables blow up and vanilla's own
blocks still could not join); ceilings now (the frame allows them in one line; not asked);
a diode facing chosen by the clicked face (a repeater's output direction is a look, as on
the floor); making `DiodeBlock.isDiode` true for the wall diodes (vanilla then reads its
own FACING property off our state and throws), so a wall repeater cannot lock a floor
repeater beside it, the one asymmetry, noted in the README.
